# Gator case

The operator first requested beat bounces with a choppy cartoon feel. A universal whole-body lift and squash read as jumping. The correction asked for knee and hip articulation, then clarified the real intent. Feet can move; the goal is a simple character-appropriate dance, with small foot taps, head bops, or knee motion.

Three subagents explored a knee pulse, hip groove, and quieter head-bop/toe-tap. The knee pulse's deepest pose felt squat-like for an idle. The hip groove had useful articulation and a heel lift, but its deep central dip and palindrome cadence felt busy. The quiet candidate preserved the relaxed crooner character best.

The first final used the quiet candidate's four drawings in order [1,0,3,2] over two beats. Low drawings1/3 landed on successive beats, relaxed0/2 on offbeats. At120BPM the cycle lasted one second and each drawing held250ms. This is essentially two small extremes with alternate shoe, head, and prop offsets. It remained an animatic with some hand, tail, microphone, and shoe drift.

The operator then clarified that evaluation can mix ideas across explorations rather than merely selecting one. The reusable process therefore includes explicit hybrid trials. Rendering differences are a reason to reconcile or redraw a hybrid, not a reason to exclude cross-candidate gestures. Gator's earlier decision does not establish that a hybrid would be worse; no hybrid drawing trial was performed in that pass.

Possible next Gator experiments include the quiet candidate's shallow knee dip with the hip candidate's heel-tap idea, or the quiet drawings with a different prop-follow rhythm. Keep the amplitude small. Judge the complete loop against the original before replacing it.

The original working canvas was640x800 with foot anchor320,776 and a64x80 logical mid-fi grid. Those were project conventions. Common registration preserved the genuinely lowered pelvis. No per-pose height normalization or whole-body transform created the dance.
