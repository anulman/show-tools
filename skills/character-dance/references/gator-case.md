# Gator: restraint and synthesis

A universal whole-body lift and squash read as jumping. The useful correction was to draw knee and hip articulation, small foot taps and head follow-through. Feet may move; plausible support matters more than a universal foot lock.

Three bounded explorations tried a knee pulse, hip groove and quieter head-bop/toe-tap. A deep knee pose read as a squat. A deep central dip with a palindrome cadence was too busy for the relaxed crooner. The quiet candidate's coherent character model worked best.

Timing made the quiet gesture useful: alternate shallow low poses on successive beats with relaxed poses on the offbeats. Four held drawings over two beats gave a choppy cartoon cadence. This is evidence from one character, not a fixed frame budget for every creature or tempo.

The operator then clarified that evaluation should mix compatible ideas. Borrowing timing can be cheap; borrowing a different anatomical gesture may need a coherent redraw. Compare an actual hybrid animatic before deciding that a single candidate is better. Differences in rendering are a reason to reconcile the drawings, not to rule out the gesture.

The original 64×80 logical grid and 640×800 registration were project conventions. Common registration preserved the genuinely lowered pelvis. Per-pose height normalization would erase that motion. Prop, shoe and face stability still required a separate polish and temporal review.
