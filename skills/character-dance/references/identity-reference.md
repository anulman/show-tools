# Approved identity and deterministic references

An animation can match its source pixels perfectly while depicting the wrong version of a character. Verify the reference relationship independently of export parity.

## Resolve the reference

Use the actual approved Figma component and edition. Record file key, node ID, edition/fidelity, raw source file SHA-256, logical dimensions, and the source-to-native transform. Download the original image or a transparent node export; an opaque canvas preview cannot establish sprite alpha. Compare any local alias to the resolved bytes. A previously approved expansion model may supersede an older avatar; do not silently revert it.

When a source is an enlarged pixel master, recover its real integer cells and registration offset. Re-expand and re-register the candidate and compare every source RGBA pixel and transparent margin. Require complete occupied-cell coverage. Do not guess offsets from the canvas size or prove identity using only cell centres. Discover the transform per asset. The Sardine correction recovered a 64×80 master from a 640×800 image at 10× with a six-pixel Y offset; these are case facts, not global defaults.

If lossless recovery is unavailable, repaint one faithful neutral in the requested style and review it beside the approved reference before creating a rig. Do not turn a failed reference extraction into permission to invent a replacement face or costume.

## Keep the model through motion

Name the rigid reference pieces and articulated chains. Extract complete pieces where possible. For each asserted rigid reuse, verify the actual source pixels, occupied coverage and transparent footprint after its declared transform; empty crops and partial stencils do not suffice. Review overlap and occlusion explicitly. Preserve source material, silhouette, body mass and defining prop shape as well as texture.

Check flexible-part and prop lengths against the approved model, not against the newly generated rig. Check cross-section thickness, taper and terminal silhouette too: a length-bounded tail can still become a thick constant-width tube near its microphone. Verify these on the actual rendered pixels, with an older failed pose as a negative when available. Include extrema and loop closure. A neutral pass does not excuse an elongated tail, changing baton length, brown replacement for a charcoal trunk, rectangular replacement for a figure-eight key, or flattened replacement shoes.

## Release certificate

Keep a compact identity record separate from the style, temporal, anatomy and export records. It contains:

- Family ID and approved reference ID, edition, file/node IDs and exact reference hash.
- Exact current combined, Body and Effects drawing hashes, or an explicit combined-only model with no separate effects.
- Identity verdict, reviewer, and a concise visible-evidence note; reference-part or proportion checks when used.

The release validator must reject missing identity approval, a failed verdict, a different reference/edition, a mismatched drawing/layer hash, and incomplete family coverage. A passing source-RGBA export record cannot satisfy this field. Revalidate a family when its source, reference or identity-defining proportions change.

Prove the validator rejects an actual past mistaken model or prop and a stale approval after a source change. Keep only minimal negative fixtures or hashes needed by a maintained check. Retain raw comparisons and movies temporarily until canonical handoff and consumer migration finish.

## Frozen rendering boundary

The certificate promotes a fixed artwork version. A deterministic renderer consumes those exact bytes, recorded poses and beat keys. It does not author or repaint artwork. Validate hashes before selection and composition; reject changed or uncertified inputs. Pixel-preserving placement/scaling and explicit presentation effects may be configured independently. A new pose, corrected model or repaint belongs in authoring, receives new hashes and fresh reviews, and is promoted before the renderer can consume it.
