# Maestro's running fall

This case records operator feedback and observed results from the November 2026 snowglobe study. It is an example, not a universal drawing count or timing preset.

## What changed the result

The operator first resolved a believable final pose. Broad alternatives exposed unnatural body shapes. The extended-prone option worked after matching the compact original model. Its head points right, its legs lie low in the snow and its staff has fallen beside it.

The requested study then needed a starting pose and three interims. The later clarification that Maestro starts running left to right mattered. A neutral model was no longer the starting pose. The earlier grounded progression looked like turning and lying down. More detail could not fix its premise.

The successful poses describe a run, support loss, an airborne fall, face-first descent and the approved landing. The operator approved those poses, then asked for a facial consistency pass. That order preserved successful motion while fixing a smaller defect.

The eye cleanup replaced broken grey clusters and gaps with simple dark shapes. The first three poses retain two readable eyes. The descent retains one eye as the skull pitches down. Consistency preserved the character through perspective instead of repeating an upright face.

The correction changed 362 native RGB pixels in four explicit facial regions. Complete comparisons found no changes to outside-region RGBA pixels, alpha, outer silhouettes, props or instance transforms. The artist updated existing components and named Body and Effects layers in place. Fresh export parity established that Figma contained the corrected pixels. Independent visual review established that the faces looked cleaner. These are separate findings.

The review MP4 uses those five drawings with held opacity swaps. It repeats twice. Each three-second cycle starts the run at zero, trip at 0.6, flight at 0.9, descent at 1.2 and landing at 1.5 seconds. The actual export is 6.1 seconds at 10 fps. The operator called the result fantastic and asked to capture the process. No additional tween drawings or runtime promotion followed from that praise.

## Failures to recognize

| Failed approach | Decision that replaced it |
| --- | --- |
| Request a whole motion and repair the resulting sequence | Establish start and end, then draw and split the middle |
| Rotate the upright sprite to suggest a fall | Draw changing shoulder, head and limb overlap through perspective |
| Keep a planted support rule during a fall | Let the body and feet leave the floor after support loss |
| Preserve palette cues while inventing new cream head shapes | Preserve readable face construction and model proportions |
| Treat an export or internal style pass as operator approval | Keep action, identity, polish, timing and operator decisions separate |
| Regenerate approved poses to clean the eyes | Make a bounded face-only edit and verify the protected pixels |
| Repeated waiting messages and authorization requests | Send one consolidated handoff and return an artifact or concrete blocker |

The operator made the sequence explicit after this study. Starting pose, ending pose, then middle poses is the default. Even a complete-motion request should be decomposed by the agent.
