# Check the hardware and latch before the full print run

Treat this as one prototype order with a pre-production fit check. The printer performs the trial locally before committing to the complete boxes and full-height bar. Darren's unchanged original is optional; skip it if his process review finds it unsuitable. Do not alter that original silently.

Use the same selected resin, orientation strategy and full wash/post-cure process for the fit pieces and final mating parts. Confirm the largest shell fits the build volume. The exact printer and resin are still to be confirmed with the fabricator.

## Check the selected real hardware

Use the purchasing BOM accompanying the revised candidate, not an approximate screw assortment. Check every distinct screw head, thread, nut and stack length once. In particular:

- The nut seats fully and its pocket stops it turning.
- The screw passes through the clearance hole without cutting a thread in the resin.
- The chosen head seats without protruding into a bearing or moving-part space.
- The screw engages the nut through its full thickness, with no end collision or bottoming.
- Tightening by hand with the selected ordinary tool clamps the joint without cracking the printed seat.
- Hardware needed for maintenance remains reachable in the assembled configuration.

A small coupon should reproduce the actual nut pocket, screw bore, head seat and material thickness. Do not print the full shells solely to discover a fastener-pocket mismatch. No coupon or revised hardware geometry is certified by this document; use only the candidate's checked and identified export set.

## Check the complete latch interfaces

Print the mating latch pieces before the long bar and large boxes. If a shortened interface sample is supplied, it is a test piece only, not a replacement bar. Complete all support removal, washing and post-curing before judging the fit.

The proposed acceptance check is 20 unloaded attach/lock/unlock/remove cycles. The count is an initial prototype check, not a durability rating. Acceptance requires:

- The parts seat by hand without forcing, uncontrolled sanding or global scaling.
- The lock engages positively and cannot release through ordinary handling or a straight pull.
- Only the intended collar/control moves; the preset grip remains adjusted.
- The selected ordinary tools reach and operate the mechanism with the bar's exterior assumed inaccessible inside the glued-in ball.
- No custom fork, tiny captive control or deep two-pin spanner is required by the minimum-tool revision.
- The retaining hardware stays with its intended component and cannot fall into the box during routine release.
- No crack, chip, binding, cross-threading or visibly increasing looseness appears during this initial cycle check.

If the fit fails, stop before the full run. Report the printer, resin, orientation, post-cure process and measured interference/looseness so the CAD or specified process compensation can be corrected. Do not scale the whole assembly to fix a hole.

## Then complete the order

Continue to the final full-height bar and boxes after the local fit check passes. Keep each part's identity and quantity, millimetre scale, protected full bar height and insertion profile. Return the process/material record and any approved local compensation with the parts.

If the actual button and spinner are available to Darren, also check their mounting interfaces, existing nuts, installed knob height and attached harness. Without those physical components, those installation fits remain based on the stated drawing dimensions and measured inputs, not a physical acceptance result.

The first assembled device still needs grip, cable-anchor, buffer/ballast-retention, free-rotation, stability and load tests with the real ball. The printer's fit acceptance does not establish a load rating or the hot-glue bond's capacity.

## Short message to send

> Please print and fully cure the hardware-fit samples and mating latch pieces first, using the actual standard screws/nuts in the BOM. Check that they seat correctly and that the latch attaches, locks and releases repeatedly with the specified ordinary tools, assuming the bar exterior is inaccessible inside the ball. If that passes, proceed with the complete boxes and full-height bar; if it does not, stop and report the fit issue before the full run.
