# Standard-hardware collar first article

This separate candidate replaces the earlier custom push-turn pin and fork tool with a purchased WDS captive M3 lock and a separate integral 4 mm hex drive. Earlier producers and published artifacts remain unchanged. This is a private geometry proposal; the operator's spinner measurements and physical first-fit checks remain outstanding.

The collar has a nominal 0.5 mm radial race gap, 0.5 mm axial race gaps and 1 mm retaining-lip overlap. Its blind drive socket is 4.35 mm across flats, 7 mm deep, with a 2 mm floor and approximately 2.49 mm minimum radial wall. These are proposed first-print allowances, not measured resin compensation or load ratings. The purchased screw's reduced neck carries no deliberate sideways drive load.

The two simpler alternatives were a hand-operated ring with a snap-in captive lock, and three direct captive screws. The former needed another support-height change and an unqualified resin snap interface; the latter required three routine lock operations. The selected collar keeps one purchased lock operation and one ordinary-key quarter turn.

## Operator's mounting contract

The bar stays permanently glued inside the disco ball. The baseplate grips the knob tightly. The removable joint couples that baseplate to the bar and must lock and unlock from below the ball. There is no separate bar-to-box retaining joint. Any stationary bearing surface is passive support, not another attachment to release.

The operator accepts either complete assembly order:

- A: preset the baseplate's knob grip, insert and lock the baseplate into the ball/bar, then seat the spinner's stock hex in the box counterbore and secure it against pullout.
- B: seat and secure the stock hex in the box first, fitting the knob and baseplate before or afterward as access permits, then secure the ball/bar to the baseplate from below.

Verify at least one complete order, including grip adjustment, knob installation, hex retention and access to every required fastener. The existing separate C2 assembly and motion checks do not establish this complete installation sequence. In particular, the knob cup can obscure top-driven hex-keeper screws. Pull-apart testing must cover the complete connection, including the knob's attachment to the spinner, rather than infer it from the hex tabs alone.

The operator reports that the stock hex's mounting height is adjustable and the knob can sit close to it. The usable range and chosen installed height remain unmeasured. The 19 mm roof-to-bar gap is a proposed latch placement, not an operator hardware requirement.

## Initial assembly

1. Prepare the separated pieces using the fabricator's selected printer and material process. Treat the original Darren bar as print-ready under the operator's instruction. The fabricator decides any print-time preparation or skipping.
2. Load the two preset cup nuts through their open channels before fitting the shoes, pads and preset screws.
3. Put the WDS screw through the separate lock cap. Wind its matched threaded washer over the screw's threaded tip onto the reduced neck, following the supplier's retention method. Seat the washer in the underside cap recess. It never passes through a finished Ø3.6 shaft bore.
4. Lower this preassembled cap axially onto the collar's open posts. Load the permanent cap-joint nut from its inner radial opening and fit the M3 × 8 button-head screw. This joint is permanent during routine use.
5. Fully retract the captive lock by 6 mm to its washer stop. Place the collar midway through its travel. Load the fixed lock nut from the back of the receiver bracket, then bring both receiver halves laterally around the collar. The midpoint position avoids sweeping an end stop into the drive boss.
6. Load the three carrier nuts through the bar's inner radial channels. Insert the receiver/collar assembly axially into the bar mouth, then fit the three M3 × 8 carrier button-head screws from outside. Complete this initial assembly before permanently gluing the ball to the bar.
7. Set the collar to release before placing the bar assembly onto the preset cup. Rotate the collar to lock with the 4 mm key, remove that key, and engage the captive M3 lock with the 2.5 mm key. Do not use the captive screw as a sideways lever.

The washer sits between a clipped lower collar shelf and the cap, with 0.5 mm total axial float. Its lowest allowed face stays 0.5 mm above the receiver lip. The cap has a 1 mm roof above that pocket. A shallow exterior cup channel gives the permanent cap-bolt tip over 0.5 mm running clearance.

The threaded washer prevents ordinary axial screw loss. It can deliberately be unwound over the threaded tip if restrained; this is not a tamper-proof or unrestricted-disassembly retention claim.

## Routine release

Move the complete assembly to the agreed service index and provide the checked underside tool space. Back the captive M3 screw out to its washer stop with the 2.5 mm key, remove that key, then turn the separate drive socket 90 degrees with the 4 mm key. Remove the key and withdraw the bar straight upward from the cup. The washer, screw, cap and collar stay with the bar.

The source drive arc is 225–315 degrees; the lock moves from source 135 degrees when locked to 45 degrees when released. The source coordinate system points positive Z downward from the bar mouth. Full key staging brings each key tip to source Z=100 mm. The 4 mm key envelope then reaches source Z=240 mm. A 160 mm temporary support lift is proposed for integration; the box checker must verify it against the actual cover and access ports.

## Fit and verification limits

At the provisional 19 mm gap, the pad contact band is 28–36 mm above the roof. Full pad engagement requires the real knob to cover that band. The three pending spinner measurements are hex across flats, hex thickness, and hex-top to knob-top height. Do not expand that request. Use the existing snug socket for the lower contact constraint. Report the 28–36 mm pad band against the measured knob-top height; installed fit remains unverified until those checks are complete.
