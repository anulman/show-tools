# Axial latch studies for visual review

B′ is a static locked-state geometry study, not a validated operating mechanism. The page shows it beside the complete original and C. A′ and D have been removed from the page. No latch is included in C's geometry-check export command.

A′ uses one over-centre slider-crank lever to pull a common annular yoke and both guided posts. A separate push-turn-lift key blocks reverse movement. Its current key-push and key-turn states intersect a taper shoe by approximately 0.374 mm³. Release is not valid. The locked pose can be inspected without claiming that the mechanism can complete an operating cycle.

B′ uses a manually lifted, quarter-turn collar that seats on fixed retaining shoulders. Its flanges capture a non-rotating draw yoke. A separate keyed pin is intended to block reverse rotation. Several discrete poses are geometrically clear, but the complete capture and release sequence, holding force, and operator access are unverified.

Both keys use a cross-lug under a keyed roof. That proposed geometry is intended to require a push and turn before withdrawal. It is not a holding-force rating, proof against every release path, or a claim that a printed key has adequate strength. The key can be removed after unlocking; no tether or full unlocked-state captivity is modelled.

The operator corrected the support surface to print-on-print, with a stationary mounting hex, an underside retaining nut, and a roughly flush bar-to-box interface. An additional upper nut is being considered, but its thread, thickness, available engagement, and resulting seating height are unconfirmed. B′'s current control is on the knob-mouth face and its proposed key withdrawal is 5.5 mm. A flush box can obstruct that motion. Actuator placement remains paused until the installed access and service sequence are resolved.

For the page, the static locked models must pass solid validity, complete body preservation, circular insertion bounds, and pairwise rigid clearance. Only the exact modelled pad compression is allowed in these locked review poses. The ordinary C/D released-pocket and sampled-motion checks remain unchanged. The page guard rejects altered review pads, and nonzero travel through the static-review path is rejected.

The separate lock follows a principle used in commercial over-centre clamps, such as [DESTACO's secondary locking mechanisms](https://www.opwces.com/content/destaco/language-masters/en/resources/how-to-buy-guides/safety-characteristics-of-destaco-manual-clamps.html). Commercial ratings do not transfer to these custom studies. Pad preload, slip, pull-off, wear, material strength, complete motion, and installed release access require separate validation before manufacturing approval.
